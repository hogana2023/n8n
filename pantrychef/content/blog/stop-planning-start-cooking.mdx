Every meal planning system asks you to do the same thing: sit down on a Sunday, decide what forty-two future meals will be, and buy accordingly. It works beautifully for about nine days.

Then a Tuesday happens. You get home late, the thing you planned needs an hour, and there is a perfectly good bag of spinach in the fridge that nobody will eat because it wasn't on the list.

## The plan is the problem

Meal planning treats your kitchen as empty. You start from a blank week and work forward to a shopping list, which means the food already on your shelves is invisible to the process. It doesn't get used because it was never part of the plan.

That's backwards. Most kitchens already contain four or five complete meals. They just aren't obvious, because the ingredients are scattered across three shelves and a freezer drawer, and nobody has the energy at 6pm to do the mental join.

## Start from the shelf, not the calendar

The alternative is to invert it. Instead of deciding what you'll eat and then buying it, look at what you have and ask what it adds up to.

This sounds like it would produce worse dinners. In practice it produces better ones, for three reasons:

- **It's honest about time.** You cook what's actually available on a Tuesday, not what a more ambitious version of you committed to on Sunday.
- **It uses things up.** Ingredients get eaten while they're good, because they're the starting point rather than an afterthought.
- **It's cheaper.** You buy the gap, not the whole meal.

The catch is that doing the join by hand is genuinely hard. Knowing that chickpeas, a lemon, some yoghurt and half a bag of spinach is a real dinner takes either a good memory or a lot of cookbooks.

## What actually changes

Once your kitchen contents are written down somewhere that can be searched, the six o'clock decision stops being a decision. You open a list, the meals you can make right now are at the top, and you cook one.

The Sunday ritual disappears. The shopping list gets shorter, because it only contains the things you don't already own. And the spinach gets eaten, which was the entire point.

> The best system is the one that still works on your worst evening of the week.

That's the test. Not whether it produces an elegant plan when you're feeling organised, but whether it survives a Tuesday.
