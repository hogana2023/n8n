Variety in cooking doesn't come from variety in shopping. It comes from a small set of ingredients that combine in a lot of directions.

Here is the list we keep coming back to. None of it is exciting on its own, which is the point.

## The ten

- Eggs
- Onions
- Garlic
- Tinned tomatoes
- Dried pasta
- Rice
- Chickpeas
- Natural yoghurt
- Lemons
- A hard cheese, usually parmesan

Add salt, oil, and whatever spices you already own, and that's the whole kit.

## Why these ten

Each one does more than one job.

**Eggs** are a protein, a binder, and a sauce. **Onions and garlic** are the base of almost every savoury thing in the European and South Asian repertoires. **Tinned tomatoes** turn into sauce, soup, stew, or braise depending only on how long you leave them alone.

**Yoghurt and lemon** are the two cheapest ways to make something taste finished. A dish that seems flat is usually short of acid, and one of those two will fix it.

## Thirty dinners, roughly

You don't need thirty recipes. You need about six techniques, each of which accepts substitutions:

- **Tomato sauce, four ways.** Garlic-forward, onion-sweet, chilli, or creamy with a spoon of yoghurt off the heat.
- **Fried rice.** Whatever protein and vegetable exist, plus egg.
- **Chickpea braise.** Tomato base, spice of your choice, yoghurt on top.
- **Omelette or frittata.** The universal solvent for leftover vegetables.
- **Rice bowl.** Rice, a cooked thing, a raw thing, an acidic thing.
- **Pasta with fat and cheese.** Cacio e pepe and its many descendants.

Six techniques times the vegetables and proteins that pass through a normal week gets you past thirty without effort, and none of them require a special trip.

## The part that's actually hard

Knowing which of the six to reach for on a given night, given what's currently in the fridge. That's the join nobody wants to do at six o'clock, and it's the reason people fall back on the same four meals.

It's also the one part a piece of software is genuinely good at.
